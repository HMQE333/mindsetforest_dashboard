Recovery & readiness

The "should I go hard today?" cluster — read these before you decide the workout.

Resting Heart Rate

Overnight · bpm

Your heart's beats per minute at complete rest, measured while you sleep. One of the simplest windows into both fitness and recovery.

↓ Trends down

Drifting from, say, 58 → 52 over weeks: a stronger, fitter heart that's well recovered.

↑ Spikes up

Suddenly 5–10 bpm over your normal one morning: poor sleep, alcohol, stress — or a bug moving in.

→ So I doOn a spiked morning, downgrade the hard session to an easy jog or rest. Water, food, early night.
✦ PayoffYou catch fatigue and illness a day or two before you feel them — and stop digging a hole you pay for all week.

HRV Status

Baseline · ms

The tiny timing differences between heartbeats overnight, compared to your own baseline — how recovered your nervous system is. Needs ~3 weeks to calibrate; one rough night won't move it, it's a trend.

↑ Balanced

Sitting in your normal range: the body is coping well. Green light for a hard effort.

↓ Unbalanced / Low

Below your range: accumulated fatigue, stress, under-sleep, or illness incoming.

→ So I doSave hard sessions for Balanced days; keep it easy or rest when it reads Low. Don't fight a Low reading.
✦ PayoffYou train hard exactly when your body can absorb it — steadier progress, far fewer burnout weeks.

Sleep Score & Stages

Nightly · 0–100

How long and how well you slept. Deep sleep repairs the body; REM restores the brain and mood. Everything else on this page depends on it.

↑ Rises

7–9 h with solid deep sleep: the recovery engine is running and every downstream number improves.

↓ Drops

Short nights or thin deep sleep: muscles and mind under-repaired — tomorrow's HRV, RHR and pace all suffer.

→ So I doTreat a low score as a signal to protect tonight: same bedtime, no screens/caffeine/alcohol late, cool dark room.
✦ PayoffSleep is the cheapest performance drug you own. Fix it and HRV, energy, focus and pace all rise — for free.

Body Battery

Live · 5–100

An "energy in the tank" gauge built from HRV, stress, sleep and activity. It charges when you rest and drains when you exert or stress — your everyday readiness dial on the 165.

↑ Wakes high

85–100 in the morning: fully charged — spend it on your hardest workout or biggest work block.

↓ Bottoms early

Flat by mid-afternoon day after day: chronic under-recovery, not just one tired afternoon.

→ So I doPut demanding tasks where the battery actually is (usually morning). If it drains fast daily: a walk, breathing, an earlier night.
✦ PayoffYou stop fighting your own physiology and schedule effort where the energy already exists.

Stress Level

Live · 0–100

Daytime "fight-or-flight" load read from your HRV — and it's not only workouts. Emails, deadlines, caffeine and bad sleep all push it up.

↓ Dips through day

Regular low-stress rest windows: a healthy, well-balanced nervous system.

↑ Pinned high

Long stretches high with no dips: your day has no recovery windows built into it.

→ So I doWhen I spot a long high stretch, insert 5 min of slow breathing, a short walk, or step off the screen.
✦ PayoffMakes invisible burnout visible before it wrecks your sleep and training. Small resets beat a big crash.

Recovery Time

Post-run · hrs (max 4d)

A countdown to when you're ready for another hard effort after a workout. On the 165 this pairs with Body Battery as your recovery read — there's no separate Training Load score here.

↑ Reads long

36–48 h+ after a session: that workout hit hard and the adaptation needs real time.

↓ Reads short

Near zero: you're fresh and cleared to load up again.

→ So I doDon't stack another hard session inside the window — fill it with easy running, mobility or rest.
✦ PayoffYou actually get fitter — because adaptation happens during recovery, not during the workout itself.
B

Fitness & output

The "am I actually getting better?" cluster — slow-moving trend lines, judged over weeks.

VO₂ Max

Trend · ml/kg/min

An estimate of your aerobic engine — how well your body pulls in and uses oxygen. Higher means fitter. It moves slowly, so judge it monthly, never daily.

↑ Climbs

Ticks up over weeks: the training is working and your aerobic base is genuinely growing.

↓ Slides

Drifts down: usually under-recovery, too much intensity, illness or heat — not lost fitness overnight.

→ So I doKeep ~80% of runs genuinely easy (talk-in-full-sentences pace) and add just one hard session a week.
✦ PayoffRace times fall, everyday effort feels easier, and your resting HR quietly drops right along with it.

Running Performance

Per run · pace · dist · kcal

The raw output of each run — plus wrist Running Power and cadence on the 165. The trick is to read pace against effort, not on its own.

↑ Pace @ lower HR

Same pace at a lower heart rate than a month ago: the single clearest sign fitness is rising.

↓ Slips / cadence drops

Pace fading at the same effort, or cadence falling as you tire: fatigue or form breaking down.

→ So I doTrack pace-at-heart-rate, aim cadence ~170–180, and pace by effort — let the numbers confirm it after.
✦ PayoffSmarter pacing → faster races and fewer injuries, because you stop redlining your easy days.

Race Predictor & Fitness Age

Trend · time · yrs

Predicted 5K–marathon times from your data, plus a "fitness age" set against your real age. Equal parts reality-check and motivation.

↓ Times / age fall

Predicted times getting quicker and fitness age dropping: the trend line points the right way.

↑ Stalls / creeps up

Predictions flat or fitness age creeping: a nudge to check consistency, sleep and load.

→ So I doSet goal-race paces off the predictor, not ego. Use fitness age as a long-term north star, not a daily number.
✦ PayoffRealistic targets you actually hit — plus a simple, motivating scoreboard for months of quiet work.
C

Everyday movement

The base layer under everything above — cheap, and easy to neglect.

Steps & Intensity Minutes

Daily · weekly ~150 min

All-day movement, plus weekly minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity. The quiet foundation that decides how fast you recover between runs.

↑ Consistent movement

Daily steps up and intensity minutes met: a strong health baseline that speeds recovery.

↓ Run then sit

Big runs but otherwise glued to a chair — the "active couch potato" trap dulls recovery.

→ So I doHold a daily step floor (~7–8k) with easy walks, and use that movement as active recovery — not more hard training.
✦ PayoffBetter recovery between runs, steadier energy and metabolic health — the cheapest fitness win there is.
